Remember the good ol' *poke button* from Facebook? It was a quirky (and often annoying) way to let someone know you were thinking about them, sometimes even sparking long-running poke battles. In a way, LinkedIn has a similar mechanic: just viewing someone's profile sends them a subtle signal.

Recently, I read that Facebook has brought the feature back to life[^1] (they made it more visible, it was never actually removed). Apparently, it lead to a large spike in poking traffic.

I figured: why not add a poke button to my own site?

### What it does
* You click *poke*
* I get a phone notification that says:
    * "Someone from [city, country] poked you!"
* That's it. No reply or anything (so no poke wars either).

### How it works
* This site is built with Jekyll (static frontend).
* The poke button sends a POST request to a small backend built with Node-RED.
* Node-RED notifies me using [Pushover](https://pushover.net/).
* On the frontend:
    * I use [localStorage](https://blog.logrocket.com/localstorage-javascript-complete-guide/) to prevent people from poking more than once per 24 hours per browser.
* On the backend:
    * I extract your IP address and look up a rough location (city-level);
    * I allow up to 3 pokes per IP per 24 hours, giving room for households or shared networks;
    * Anything beyond that is blocked with a friendly error.


### But why?
Mostly because it was a fun and small build. Also because I really like this kind of features that makes the internet feel a bit more human and not too serious (it is a _personal_ website after all!).
